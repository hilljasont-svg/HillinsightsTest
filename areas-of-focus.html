Areas of Focus

My titles have included engineer, project manager, manager, and consultant, but much of the work required the same underlying practice: understanding how work actually occurred, identifying gaps between intent and execution, and helping people develop practical paths forward.

01

Organizational Alignment

Connecting priorities, roles, decisions, and expectations so people can move in the same direction without relying on constant correction.

You may experience

Leaders communicate what needs to happen but provide different explanations for why, who holds authority, or what takes priority. Teams then interpret the same direction differently, decisions circle through multiple people, and cross-functional work depends more on individual relationships than shared expectations.

How I approach similar conditions

Alignment work begins by comparing stated priorities with the patterns people encounter in daily work. I use interviews, facilitated conversations, document review, and process mapping to understand how leaders frame the work, how employees interpret it, and where authority or expectations become unclear. The goal does not center on forcing agreement. The goal centers on making differences visible enough for the organization to address them directly.

From there, I help clarify decision responsibilities, operating expectations, information needs, and the reasoning behind the chosen direction. Cross-functional collaboration becomes part of the work whenever priorities cross organizational boundaries. Leaders retain ownership of the decisions while Hill Insights supports the process used to reach and communicate them.

In practice

Translating requirements across organizational boundaries

During a complex system-development effort, operational users, engineering teams, installation groups, and program leadership depended on one another while working from different responsibilities and constraints. I translated operational requirements across those groups, clarified what each group needed from the others, and maintained alignment from early development through installation and testing. The work contributed to a functioning capability delivered across four operational platforms.

  • Stakeholder interviews
  • Facilitated dialogue
  • Role and decision clarification
  • Cross-functional process review

02

Process, Systems & Technology

Understanding how work actually moves before asking a new tool, policy, or procedure to improve it.

You may experience

Important work depends on memory, informal workarounds, or knowledge held by one or two people. A technology platform may capture information without supporting the decisions people need to make. Handoffs create delays, duplicate effort appears across teams, and written procedures no longer match what employees actually do.

How I approach similar conditions

As a process consultant, I start with the work rather than the tool. I listen to the people performing it, observe the sequence they follow, identify the information and decisions required at each point, and compare formal procedures with actual practice. That examination often reveals whether friction comes from the process, the supporting system, unclear ownership, competing requirements, or some combination of all four.

Technology enters the conversation after the organization understands what the work requires. Hill Insights can then help connect business needs to workflow design, governance, training, reporting, and implementation choices. The organization keeps control of technical decisions. My role focuses on helping people understand the operational consequences attached to those decisions.

In practice

Turning individual knowledge into repeatable practice

A member-based organization relied heavily on knowledge held by a small staff. Through interviews, direct participation in the work, workflow review, and process consultation, I helped translate that knowledge into repeatable practices across member onboarding, billing, events, and communication. The work clarified handoffs, connected separate activities through a shared member journey, and created a foundation for greater continuity when responsibilities or personnel change.

  • Current-state process mapping
  • Workflow observation
  • Requirements clarification
  • Operational continuity planning

03

Transition & Transformation

Helping organizations understand what must change, what must continue, and what people need as they move between the two.

You may experience

Growth, restructuring, leadership turnover, or a major technology effort creates movement without a shared understanding of the destination. Employees hear what will change and how implementation will occur, but not why leadership selected that path. Old responsibilities remain active beside new ones, risks receive uneven attention, and uncertainty encourages people to create their own explanations.

How I approach similar conditions

Transition work begins by defining the requirement behind the movement. I help leaders examine the risks, constraints, responsibilities, dependencies, and organizational habits connected to the proposed direction. That work separates the visible initiative from the deeper transformation it may require across leadership behavior, operating processes, workforce expectations, customer relationships, and supporting technology.

I do not direct employees or take ownership of the organization’s decisions. I help create the conditions for people to examine what the transformation asks of them, surface competing interpretations, and sequence the work in a way that acknowledges operational reality. Progress comes from clearer requirements and responsibilities, not urgency for its own sake.

In practice

Connecting separate problems through one transformation path

An organization faced technology instability, leadership misalignment, employee retention concerns, process friction, and declining customer trust. Rather than treating each concern as an independent project, I developed a phased diagnostic and intervention strategy that connected them through one transformation path. The resulting sequence established leadership alignment and organizational diagnosis before technology redesign, team development, workforce planning, and customer-trust work moved forward.

  • Readiness and risk review
  • Action Research
  • Stakeholder engagement
  • Phased intervention planning
