Experience

My career began in engineering, project management, and operational support for complex naval submarine systems. Much of that work occurred between organizational boundaries.

Operational users, engineers, installation teams, program leaders, and other stakeholders often approached the same effort with different responsibilities, constraints, and definitions of success. My role frequently involved listening across those groups, translating requirements, clarifying expectations, identifying where work or information broke down, and helping people develop a shared path forward.

Graduate study in Organization Development and Change gave me the language, research, and disciplined methods to understand that much of my earlier work already reflected OD principles. Process consultation, action research, systems thinking, organizational diagnosis, and collaborative inquiry provided formal frameworks for practices I had developed through experience.

That education did not replace my engineering and project-management background. It helped me understand where organization development fits within it. Today, I bring those areas together intentionally, helping organizations examine the relationship between people, processes, technology, and structure before committing to a solution.
