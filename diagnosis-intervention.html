Graduate Case Proposal

From Organizational Diagnosis to Intervention Design

A consulting proposal showing how diagnostic evidence, stakeholder communication, and evaluation can guide a coordinated portfolio of organization development interventions.

Overview

This graduate consulting proposal addressed a case organization experiencing strategic ambiguity, fragmented enterprise-system practices, cross-functional misalignment, uneven leadership capability, workforce-retention concerns, and declining customer trust. The assignment required a defensible path from entry and contracting through diagnosis, intervention design, implementation planning, communication, and evaluation.

Question Examined

How could an OD engagement help the organization understand the relationships among leadership, strategy, processes, people, technology, and customer outcomes before committing to interventions? The proposal treated the presenting issues as signals within an open system rather than independent problems assigned to separate departments.

Methodology and Design

The Action Research model provided the overall structure. Entry and contracting established scope, authority, roles, access, and expectations. Diagnostic work then used the Harrison Open Systems Model to examine inputs, strategy, technology, structures, processes, people, culture, and outputs. Data collection combined document review, employee interviews, focus groups, leadership discussions, and available organizational measures.

The proposal did not assume that the case description supplied enough evidence to prescribe a final solution. Intervention choices remained contingent on diagnostic findings and joint interpretation with the client.

Proposed Intervention Portfolio

Leadership Alignment and Strategic Clarity

Structured leadership work would clarify priorities, decision rights, and the rationale behind major change paths without assuming that agreement from every stakeholder represented a realistic goal.

ERP Governance, Reporting, and Training Redesign

This intervention connected system use with governance, reporting expectations, business processes, and role clarity. Training would follow agreed processes rather than compensate for unresolved operating decisions.

Cross-Functional Process Alignment and Team Development

Facilitated process mapping and team development would surface handoffs, competing assumptions, and breakdowns that individual departments could not resolve alone.

Leadership Development Training

Leadership development would focus on observable practices tied to diagnosed needs, supported by practice, feedback, and follow-through.

Human Capital Retention and Employee Development Planning

The proposed work would clarify development pathways, manager responsibilities, employee expectations, and conditions contributing to retention risk.

Customer Trust Recovery Planning

This intervention would connect internal operating improvements with external commitments, communication, recovery measures, and evidence that customer concerns influenced decisions.

Communication and Escalation

The plan established reciprocal reporting between Hill Insights and the client. Regular status reports would summarize progress, decisions, risks, dependencies, and upcoming work. The client would provide access, responses, decisions, and validation through named points of contact. Two-way escalation paths distinguished routine coordination from issues requiring sponsor or executive action.

Schedule and Boundaries

The conceptual schedule began with entry and contracting in August 2026, continued through diagnosis in December, and moved into interventions after the year-end holidays. It intentionally stopped before final exit activities because exit requirements depend on findings, approved interventions, implementation progress, and demonstrated client capability.

Evaluation Strategy

Evaluation used Kirkpatrick's levels to distinguish participant response, learning, behavior, and organizational results. Measures varied by intervention rather than forcing every activity into the same instrument. Where appropriate, a Phillips ROI analysis could translate benefits, costs, risk reduction, or avoided losses into financial terms.

Professional Reflection

The proposal reinforced the need to separate a complete consulting design from the false certainty of a predetermined solution. A credible proposal can define methods, governance, decision points, candidate interventions, and evaluation logic while remaining honest about what cannot yet be known.
